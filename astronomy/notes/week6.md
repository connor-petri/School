## Fusion and Fission
### Fission
- The splitting of atoms which results in the release of energy.
- Nuclear power plants and bombs work this way.

### Fusion
- The fusing of atoms which results in the release of energy.
- Stars fuse elements which is responsible for the energy they release.
- This is how stars produce their energy.
- We are working on developing power plants that use fusion, but we have yet to crack the challenge of getting more energy out of fusion than we put in.
- If the particles are not going fast enough, they are deflected by the electric force that repels particles with like-charges.
- In order for fusion to occur, they must be traveling fast enough that the repelling force doesn't have enough time to push it out of the way.

#### Iron
Iron acts as a sort of "dividing line" on the periodic table. You cannot get nuclear energy from iron. 
- Everything after iron on the periodic table had elements with a high atomic mass. We can split these elements to get the nuclear energy from the molecule. 
- Everything before iron on the periodic table has low atomic mass. We can fuse these elements to access their nuclear energy.

### Einstein's Mass-Energy Equation
Einstein's Mass-Energy Equation relates the total energy within an object to its mass.

$$
E = mc^2
$$

Where $E$ is the energy an object has, $m$ is the mass of the object, and $c$ is the speed of light.

## Sun Layers
- **Corona** - The area above the "surface" of the sun.
- **Photosphere** - The visual "surface" of the sun. Also the coldest part of the sun.
- **Convection Zone** - Beneath the photosphere, giant convection currents churn as mass is heated by the core, rises, cools off near the photosphere, and sinks again.
- **Radiation Zone** - This is where EMR gets trapped, almost endlessly bouncing off of the densely packed matter in this layer. Theoretically, photons could be trapped in here until the star dies if it is unlucky enough.
- **Core** - Where matter is fused, releasing a massive amount of energy. This can happen due to the extremely high temperature and pressure at the core.
