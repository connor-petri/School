## Orbits in Space
An object can enter orbit around another body by having sufficient tangential velocity to process around the body rather than colliding with it. Think of this as going fast enough sideways that you miss the ground when gravity pulls you down.

## Formation of Large Bodies
### Star-Forming Clouds
- Stars form in dark clouds of dusty gas in interstellar space.
- The gas between the starts is called the **interstellar medium**.

#### Interstellar Reddening
- Stars viewed through the edges of the cloud look redder because dust blocks *shorter-wavelength* blue tight more effectively than *longer-wavelength* red light.

### Accretion Disks
When a star is formed, much of the dust around it is captured into its orbit. This dust turns into several disks after some time. These are called **accretion disks** and they are where planetary bodies form. The dust in these disks slowly accumulates into balls of gas and rock over time. The more mass these balls get, the more mass they pull in from the disks. Eventually these balls become large enough to be asteroids, comets, dwarf planets, and planets.

Accretion disks all orbit the same direction around the star, which results in the planetary bodies formed from these disks to also orbit the same direction around the star.

## The Sol Star System
- **Sol** - Our Sun.
- **Terrestrial Planets** - Rocky planetary bodies.
    - Mercury
    - Venus
    - Earth
    - Mars
- **The Asteroid Belt** - Leftover rocks from the formation of the terrestiral planets.
- **Gas Giants** - Large bodies consisting of mostly gas
    - Jupiter
    - Saturn
    - Uranus
    - Neptune
- **Kuiper Belt** - A disk of comets beyond the orbit of Neptune
- **Dwarf Planets** - Too big to be a moon and too small to clear it's orbit of other objects.
    - Ceres
    - Pluto
    - Haumea
    - Makemake
    - Eris
- **Oort Cloud** - A spherical shell of icy objects in the outer solar system.
