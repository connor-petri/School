## Useful Databases and Article Repositories
- **Google Scholar**: A widely used search engine for scholarly literature across various disciplines. It provides access to articles, theses, books, and conference papers.
- **ScienceDirect**: A leading full-text scientific database offering journal articles and book chapters from more than 2,500 peer-reviewed journals and over 33,000 books.
- **Academic Publisher Websites**: Many academic publishers (e.g., Elsevier, Springer, Wiley) have their own websites where you can search for and access research articles.
- **University Libraries**: Most universities provide access to a wide range of databases and journals through their library services. This can include access to subscription-based journals and interlibrary loan services.
- **IEEE Explore**: A digital library for research in engineering and technology, providing access to journals, conference proceedings, and standards published by the IEEE.
- **arXiv.org**: A preprint repository for research papers in physics, mathematics, computer science, and related fields. It allows researchers to share their work before formal peer review.

### Refining searches with Boolean Operators
- **AND**: Narrows the search by combining terms. For example, "climate change AND renewable energy" will return results that include both terms.
- **OR**: Broadens the search by including either term. For example, "climate change OR global warming" will return results that include either term.
- **NOT**: Excludes terms from the search. For example, "climate change NOT policy" will return results that include "climate change" but exclude those that also include "policy".
- **Quotation Marks**: Use quotation marks to search for an exact phrase. For example, "climate change impacts" will return results that include that exact phrase

### Introduction
- Outline the state of the field
- Define the topic of the article
- Explain why the topic is important
- Outline the methodology that will be used
- Rehearse the main points of the conclusion

### The Text
- Familiarize yourself with key technical terms
- Read strategically, not necessarily in order
- Don't get hung up on a sentence you don't understand. Move on and come back to it later.
- Annotate the text as you read. Write down questions, comments, and connections to other work in the margins.
