## The Provisioning Hypothesis
The **provisioning hypothesis** proposed to *infer* mating systems and sexual psychologies from locomotion.
- Coined by **C. Owen Lovejoy** 
- Bipedalism -> hands free -> food transport -> regular provisioning.
- **Provisioning** boosts female reproductive success -> favors **monogamous pair-bond**.
- Investment payoff hinges on **paternal certainty; pair-bonding/mate-guarding** raise expected genetic returns.
- **Outcomes:** 
    - Reduced male-male competition, shifts in **sexual dimorphism**.
    - Pair-bonds stabilize, one to few females (tendency towards polygyny).
    - **Family unit** becomes key selection arena; **sexual division of labor (SDL)** emerges as complementary roles.

#### Energetics and Life History: Why Provisioning Could Pay
- Maternal constraints: **pregnancy, lactation,** infant transport -> reduced foraging radius.
- **Distance-payload** trade-off: male transport adds **calories;** predicable **home base** or rendezvous point -> **sexual division of labor.**
- **Potential effects:** shorter interbirth intervals, higher infant survivorship, paternal certainty, pair-bonding. 

#### How Provisioning Connects to Sexual Division of Labor
- **Sexual division of labor** as adaptive complementarity: male **procure/transport**, female **gestate/lactate/care**
- **Visibility effect:** procurement looks **decisive/public;** care looks **continuous/private**

### Afterlife of the Provisioning Hypothesis
- **Late 90's-2010's:** confidence **erodes** as single-cause for bipedalism; multiple alternatives + evidence questioned.
- **2009-2010 "Ardi" papers:** pair-bond provisioning resurgence, but no wide return to consensus.
- **Provisioning** -> one pathway of many; **pair-bond** narrative remains **repackaged** in popular texts
    - **Example:** Gray & Garcia textbook foregrounds the pair-bond.

#### Why the Pair-Bond Persists
- Fits cultural **biases;** offers a neat causal chain.
- **Sticky Stories:** simple, memorable, and "stick" in minds.
- **Zombie theories:** critiqued, but refuted to die. (*Man the Hunter*, Laetoli couple dimorphism).


### Feminist Critique: What Provision Smuggles In
- Treats **family (heterosexual pair + dependent child) as the **selection unit**.
- Moves from **what can be done** (transport) -> to **who must do it** (men).

#### Parental Certainty
- Assumes **paternal certainty** to justify costly male investment.
- **Cross-cultural variation:** paternal investment ranges from **intense direct care** to **indirect** support or **olow** presence.
    - **Decenter paternal certainty;** why make **assured paternity** the gateway to infant food when **cooperative care** already moves calories?
    - Paternity assurance can **constrain** women's autonomy; ignores **networked** resource flows.

#### Fossils
- **Dimorphism & fossils:** small, biased samples;
reverse inference risks; multiple social ecologies fit the same straits.
    - **Laetoli $\neq$ social data:** footprints show gait, not sex/kinship/pair-bonding.
    - **Bipedalism** -> provisioning -> monogamy not uniquely implied.

## Are We Monogamous?
- Hunams show **faculative pair-bonding**, but also **multiple** marriage/mating patterns; wide cultural variations.
    - ~75% modern humans are culturally monogamous.
- What is a pair-bond? That is contested.
- Typology of different human mating patterns, from monogamy to polyamorous to visiting unions.

### Schacht & Kramer's Argument
- Humans show **facultative pair-bonding**: monogamy common, but **not** exclusive or universal.
- Variation tracks **ecology, demography, and institutions**.
- Evolved human sexuality can be different from cultural sexuality.
- Consider **when and why** a mating form/marriage pattern is favored.
- Culturally and institutionally enforced.

#### Why Forms Shift
1. **Ecology/Resources:** clumped/defensible vs dispersed/unpredictable.
2. **Demographics**
    - **Mating market:** sex ratios, age gaps, migration.
    - **Childcare logistics:** are alloparents or kin-networks available or distant?
3. **Institutions:** Inheritance, bride wealth/dowry, religion, **state enforcement** of monogamy.

### Terminology
- **Monogamy** - a pair-bonded coupling pattern or marriage between two spouses.
    - not sex or gender specific, but might be defined by state/legal/religious practices.
    - life-long or **Serial;** divorce can be common.
    - **Serial Monogamy** - long pair-bonds over time without overlap; most common.
        - sequential spouses
        - divorce
    - **Monogamy/Serial Monogamy** - common where institutions enforce it; high divorce/remarriage rates.
    - **Social Monogamy** - public identified pair/unit; structures household labor, childcare, inheritance, but may include serial unions.
    - **Genetic Monogamy** - exclusive reproduction with one partner, as evidenced in offspring.
    - Extra-pair sex $\neq$ collapse of pair-bond; think **tolerance,** secrecy, or cyclical dynamics.
    - Expect **mismatch** across settings; measure both when possible.

- **Polygamy** - umbrella term for multiple partners, multiple spouses/husbands/wives, not sex or gender specific.
    - **Polygyny** - One man, multiple wives.
    - **Polyandry** - One woman, multiple husbands.
    
- **Visiting/Duolocal Unions**
    - **Visiting/duolocal** - temporary unions between partners, relationships are flexible and they often ive in separate households.
    - **Visiting unions:** decouple mating from co-residence; usually matrilineal, matriarchal, and matrilocal; maternal kin support for childcare.
        - Property rights, inheritance, power, and provision resides with women only.

## Marriage System Videos

### Video 1: Marriage in America
- Decline in marriage in general.
- Women don't need as much support than they have in the past.
- Women's suffrage has given women rights to do things themselves without their husband's permission.
- Women are 2x as likely to file for divorce.
- Marriage rates go down as economic inequality goes up.
- People are waiting longer to get married.
- Marriages are being treated as the end goal/capstone of a relationship.
- Less social pressure. Only 1/10 Americans say you must be married to have a happy life.

### Video 2: Polygyny in the Massai of Africa
- Male has livestock.
- Male has 3-5 wives.
- These are not arranged marriages.
- Passing on the family name is highly valued.
- Largely a homesteading lifestyle.
- Women are married off significantly younger than is legal in the U.S.
- Parents "give away" their daughters. Happens at 15 years old.
- Women often perpetuate the idea of multiple wives for extra help or as a surrogate mother.
- Wives have a sort of sister relationship with each other.

### Video 3: Fraternal Polyandry in the Himalayas
- Familial marriage arrangements.
- Wife automatically marries her husband's brothers.
- Resource scarcity makes this practical.
- Limits the number of babies.
- The wife has to schedule sex and manage jealousy.
- Patriarchal society.
- Education is reducing this practice.

### Video 4: Walking Marriages in the Mouso of Southwest China
- Matriarchal Society.
- Tibetan Buddhism
- Women can have as many boyfriends as they please during their lives.
- No father role.
- Men visit the women for a night then return to their homes.
- Women have the choice whether or not to practice monogamy.
- Women are the breadwinners.
- Men assist their sisters with their childcare.
- Men were still warriors.
