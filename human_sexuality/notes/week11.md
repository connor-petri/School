## Grandmother Hypothesis (cont.)
### Comparative menopause
- **Traditional Menopauseal mammals:** orcas, false killer-whales, and short finned pilot whales.
    - **Others based on new evidence:** belugas and narwhals, macaques, maybe elephants (under review)
- Evolutionary rarity among mammals

### Orcas vs Humans
- **File History Comparisons:**
    - **Orca females** mature around 10-13, stop calving near 40, and often live up to about 90.
    - **Human females** mature around 12 (average), last births early 40s, average lifespan 81 years old.
- Long-term studies show reproduction stops while bodies keep aging slowly; hallmark of **menopause**
    - In these whale, males die younger, ofter near 30-40; survival drops ofter mother dies
    - Orca grandmothers lead, hunt, and provision kin; sons rely heavily on maternal support.

#### Why do Human Males Keep Living?
- Later-life male sexuality exists, but hormonal change is gradual not abrupt
- Cooperative breeding "helper" roles
    - Roles include paternal, avuncular and grandfather care in many settings
- **Key question:** do older men's contributions make longevity adaptive?

#### Andropause
- framed as late-onset *hypogonadism* with gradual, variable testosterone decline, not abrupt male menopause.
- **Fertility also ages:** semen volume and mobility fall; DNA damage rises; affects conception.
    - Obesity and chronic disease can exaggerate hormone decline
- Later-life men ofter shift effort into teaching, provisioning and childcare; impacts depend on residence, cultural contexts, and power relations

#### Grandfathers and Complementarity? 
- Older men can contribute childcare, provisioning, teaching, conflict mediation, and support for social learning
- **Maternal grandmothers** = better child outcomes and shorter birth intervals; fathers and male kin also care and provision
- Few studies tie grandfather care to child outcomes; historic records show *negative effects*
- **Biology suggests capacity for male investment,** but cultural norms downplay male care and limit data.

### Alloparents and cooperative breeding:
- Descendant success increases as the pool of potential caregivers/alloparents expands
- Menopause implies matralinial societies
