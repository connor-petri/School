## Measuring Sexuality Across Cultures
- **Measuring sexuality** across cultures **is hard:** access, comparability and projection of western values complicate things.
- **Method:** start with **emic** (local terms/insider meanings), translate to **etic** (outsider viewpoint) for comparison.

Doing this avoids category collapse. Projecting or mapping other cultures onto western values causes data loss and inaccuracies.

### Analytical Categories (not identities)
- **Body assignment at birth:** AFAB/AMAB
- **Gender identitiy:** emic/self-understood (man, woman, both, neither, other, ect.)
- **Social Rule:** Societal Roles
- **Sexual behavior** 
- **Desire:** Sexual orientation

### Orientation $\neq$ identitiy $\neq$ role
- Orientation = desire; identity = emic naming; role = social position.
- **Role and recognition** may outweigh inner identity.
- **Risk of category errors if collapsed**

## Origin of Sexual Anthropology
### Bronislaw Malinowski
- Studied the people of the **Trobriand Islands**
- Argued sexual psychology is culturally patterned and not universal. 
- Posthumous diaries revealed racist/sexist/private remarks; sparked major debates on reflexivity, ethics, and the myth of neutral observers.

### Margaret Mead
- Mead's *Coming of Age in Samoa* (1928):
    - popularized **cultural relativism** for U.S. audiences; questioned Euro-American sexual customs.
    - argued adolescence and sexual experience are **Culturally constructed.**
    - **Female agency:** Samoan girls cavitate kin-supervised flirtation and peer sociality.

#### Video: Margaret Mead and the Origins of Sexual and Gender Identity
- Lived in Samoa for a long period of time.
- Fascinated by their attitude towards sex.
- Was involved with men and women in her own life.
- Studied her own culture during WWII because she couldn't access the pacific.
- Became a professor.

### Ruth Benedict
- **Gender Plurality focus:** documented Greal Plains Native Americans and other cases of **third gender roles** linked to spiritual power/guardian spirits; popularized a cultural reading of sexuality/gender plurality.
    - The proper term for someone that has this "third gender" is **two-spirited** which was coined by Native American activists in the 1990's
- Argued sexual meanings are embedded in a society's wider cultural patterns.

### We'wha
- A two-spirited Native American potter, artist, weaver, ritual specialist.
- Acted as a Native American delegate to Washington D.C., a role reserved for two-spirited individuals.

## South Asia/India: Panthi / Kothi (role based sexual categories)
- **AMAB role system: panthi** (typically penetrative "men"/tops) **kothi** (feminized/receptive/bottoms)
- Identities are **gendered roles,** not Western "gay" or "bisexual" categories.
- **Kothi** presentations varies; may identify ad men or something like non-binary/third gender, often not as women.
- Public health labels miss cultural distinctions.
- **Hijra:** distinct, socially recognized third gender; relation to kothi/panthi categories can overlap, but usually distinct.
    - **AMAB or intersex;** feminine presentation; not "women": **Hijra** is its own gender category
    - **Ritual authority:** blessings at births/weddings; in some traditions the power to curse.
    - **Recognition & initiation** legitimacy shaped by community norms.
    - **Sexuality Fluid:** some partner with men; others practice celibacy.

### Video: Hijra

- Their songs detail the struggles they face.
    - Often relegated to the fringes of society.
    - They face harassment that hasn't waned in years.
    - The popularity of the band is the first time they have seen change in how they are treated by society.
- They do not identify as a part of the LGBTQ+ community.

## Central and West Africa: Spiritualized Sexual Identities
- **Across Central Africa and West Africa:** certain individuals recognized as spirit mediums/healers; involves cross-gender presentation and same-sex relations in some contexts
- **Examples:**
    - **Bodeme** of the Dagara people of Ghana are gay men/lesbian women/bisexual non-binary spirit mediators.
    - Central African **jinbandaas** healers; cross-gender attire/behaviors can be part of the ritual costume of the medium.
- **Colonial/missionary impacts:** imported moral codes and anti-sodomy laws reframed practices; postcolonial states maintain uneven enforcement.
    - Ritual respect coexists with everyday stigma or danger

## Samoa: Fa'afafine ("in the way of a woman)
- **In Polynesia,** third genders and forth genders are common; in *Samoa,** third gender called **Fa'afafine**
- Recognized social identity for **AMAB** people with feminine or fluid presentation.
- Embedded in **family/community work;** caregiving household tasks, cultural performance; role sometimes (rarely) assigned in all-boy households to distribute gendered labor.
- Ofter partner with a cisgender man; relationships **between fa'afafine** are uncommon. Men who partner with fa'afafine may not adopt Western LGBTQ+ labels.
