## Human life-history theory:
- finite energy budgets; allocation across growth, maintenance, sex, and reproduction shapes age at first birth, total fertility and lifespan
- Human strategy is slow: extended childhood and adolescence
- **Children are Costly:** large brain w/ high metabolic demand
- **Tradeoffs:** lactation suppresses ovulation; illness or fool shortfall lengthens birth intervals; help from others shortens them

### Humans among Primates
- Humans have longer childhoods
- Need more care than other primate offspring
- Other primates move towards independence earlier with less mentoring
- Human childhood assumes extended teaching and training/practice
- How do older adults fit into society once they are done reproducing?

#### Life-History: Chimps vs Humans
- **Fertility vs survival:** humans stop midlife yet live long. Chimps cycle into their forties then die soon after.
- **Cycles:** humans have year-round sexuality; chimps show visible swellings that focus mating on fertile days.
    - humans average 28 days; chimps average about 35-36 days
- **Birth-spacing:** humans can have long nursing-related gaps shaped by help and work; chimps space births widely with care centered on the mother alone.
    - Humans: 1-4 years
    - Chimps: 6 years
- **Last births and menopause:** humans often have last births in the late thirties or early forties, menopause near fifty, decades of elder life; chimps have last births near forty with little post-menopausal lifespan.

### Menopause
- **Menopause** is the end of ovulatory cycling.
    - Clinically marked when *twelve months pass* without **menstruation** and the cause is not pregnancy or lactation
    - **median age:** 50 with population variation
    - **perimenopause:** Time leading up to menopause
- **Ovarian aging** causes menopause
    - **oocytes** (immature eggs) count decreases through **atresia**
- **Declining fertility before the endpoint:** conception probability drops in the early to mid 40s. miscarriage risk climbs; last births cluster in the early forties in subsidence cultures.
- **Menopause** is extremely rare in mammals:
    - treaded as a biological enigma in humans
    - The **Grandmother Hypothesis**

### The Grandmother Hypothesis
- The **Grandmother Hypothesis** proposed that humans evolved long lifespans after menopause because grandmothers who helped feed and care for their grandchildren increased the survival of their descendants, which indirectly passed on the grandmothers' genes.
- Coined in the 1990s by Hawkes, O'Connell, and Blurton-Jones

#### Hadza Field Study
- **Hadza** (Tanzania) ethnographic field study of foodways
    - **Methods:** timed forays, first-out and last-back logs, ages recorded, loads weighed at return
    - **Findings:** older women left camp early, came back late, carried heavy loads of tubers, berries, and nuts while big game was unreliable
        - **weaned children** ate those steady foods and did better across weaning periods. 

#### Video: Why Your Grandma is an Evolutionary Mystery
- The existence of grandmothers is super weird, only a few other species have them
- **The Grandmother Hypothesis**
    - Long post-reproductive lifespan allows old people to invest in the reproductive success of their offspring's children
    - More labor = more food
- Cant they just do this while also retaining fertility?
    - Not juggling both sets of offspring increases effectiveness
- **Problem** all mammals *can* survive long enough to become grandparents
    - It is very hard to survive long enough (i.e. avoiding being eaten)
    - Grandmothers *may* be a side effect of our tool use and being the top of the food chain.

### Side Effects of Menopause
- More brittle bones
- Sexual function reduces

### Main Critique of Grandmother Hypothesis
- **Early critique:** guppy study extended lifespan and reproduction stopped, but fish are not humans.
- **Captive primate examples:** long lives from steady food, veterinary care, medicines create late-life cycle changes not seen in wild groups.
    - captivity artifact; not species wide
- **Oopause vs menopause**
    - **oopause:** decline in ovarian function and reserves, fertility drops with senescence, many wild mammals die soon after
    - **menopause:** year without menses, district hormone shift, long natural life afterward in many ecologies.
- Captivity narratives weak; people are haters; a long human post-reproductive span is rare across mammals and makes sense where elders turn time and labor into better outcomes.

### How do we know the Grandmother Hypothesis is Sound?
- **Three+ ways**
1. **Ethnographic field studies**
2. **Historical demography/agrarian studies**
3. **Comparitive hormone studies**
- **Other methods** mathematical and statistical modeling and genetic studies.
