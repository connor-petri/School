\subsection{Counting Subsets of a Set: Combinations}
\hrulefill
\subsubsection*{r-Combination}
The number of ways of unorderly selection of $r$ elements taken from a set of  $n$ elements.\\
If $n, r \in \mathbb{Z} \land 1 \leq r \leq n$, then the number of $r$-combinations of a set of $n$ elements is
given by the formula:
\begin{equation*}
    \binom{n}{r} = \frac{n!}{r!(n - r)!}
\end{equation*}

\paragraph*{Example}
The number of 16-byte strings that contain at least 13 'i's is given by:
\begin{align*}
    \binom{16}{13} + \binom{16}{14} + \binom{16}{15} + \binom{16}{16} &= \frac{16!}{13!3!} + \frac{16!}{14!2!} + \frac{16!}{15!1!} + \frac{16!}{16!0!} \\
    &= 560 + 120 + 16 + 1 \\&= 697
\end{align*}

\paragraph*{Example}
Two new drugs are to be tested on a group of 60 lab rats. 22 mice are to be given each drug and 16 are left as a control.
How many ways can the assignment of treatments to mice be made?
\begin{align*}
    &\binom{60}{22} \cdot \binom{38}{22} \cdot \binom{16}{16}\\
    =& 3.148e26
\end{align*}

\subsubsection*{Permutations with Sets of Indistinguishable Objects}
Suppose a collection consists of $n$ objects of which
\begin{itemize}
    \item $n_1$ are of type 1 and are indistinguishable from each other.
    \item $n_2$ are of type 2 and are indistinguishable from each other.
    \item $\cdots$
    \item $n_k$ are of type n and are indistinguishable from each other.
\end{itemize}
and suppose that $n_1 + n_2 + \cdots + n_k = n$, then the jumber of distinguishable permutations of the
$n$ objects is given by:
\begin{equation*}
    \frac{n!}{n_1!n_2! \dots n_k!}
\end{equation*} 




\paragraph*{Example}
A bag contains 5 red, 3 blue, and 2 green marbles. How many different ways can the marbles be arranged in a row?
\begin{align*}
    &\frac{10!}{5!3!2!} \\
    =& 2520
\end{align*}
