## Technical Communications: Conventions and Characteristics

Technical writing is a genre of nonfiction the encompasses technical material and writing produced in day to day business communication. It's purpose is to shape and transform information. It's ultimate goal is to be user friendly.

### Purposes of Technical Writing
- Conveying technical or specialized information in an accessible and usable way
- Conveying clear instructions on how to do something
- Conveying information that advances the goals of the company or organization

### Technical Writing Conventions
Every genre of writing has unique characteristics and rules that help readers classify a document as belonging to a particular genre. Technical writing is no different.

#### Audience
- Consider the knowledge base of the people you are writing for
- Consider what your audience will be doing with the information you are giving them
    - **Primary Audience** are the people for which the techincal documentation is intended for
    - **Secondary Audience** are the people who are "going to be CCed in the email", or people who might skim the writing
    - **Tertiary Audience** are the people who might get "forwarded the email" or may have little investment in learning the information presented

#### Writing Style
- Consider cultural conventions
- Consider profession conventions
- Highly dependent on what you and your audience are doing and what tools are being used to accomplish those goals (i.e. programming language)
- Often short, consice sentences and paragraphs.
- Purpose is clear from the start
- Use active voice

#### Format
- How the information is presented
- Highly dependent on what you and your audience are doing and what tools are being used to accomplish those goals (i.e. programming language)
- Use established standards where possible

#### Structure
- How the information is presented
- Often short, consice sentences and paragraphs
- Use esablished standards where possible, such as javadoc or markdown
- Clear transitions and structural queues, such as headings and subheadings
- Use of tables, figures, charts, etc.
- IEEE style guide - derived from Chicago

#### Tone
- Which words and language are used
- Highly dependent on audience
- Business/professional in tone, where the level of formality depends on your relationship to the audience
- Courteous and constructive

### Qualities of Technical Writing (in order of importance)
- Reliable Evidence/Data
- Clarity of Expression
- Sound Reasoning
- Good Structure
- Sound Rhetoric
- Readable Prose
- Correct Referencing and Citations
- Lack of Mechanical Errors
