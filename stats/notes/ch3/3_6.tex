\subsection{Poisson Probability Distribution}
\hrulefill
\paragraph*{Definition:}
The \textbf{Poisson distribution} is used to model the number of times a rare event occurs.\\
Examples:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The number of earthquakes in CA in a month.
    \item The number of misprints on a page of some document.
    \item The number of atoms emitted by a radioactive particle.
\end{itemize}

A random variable $X$ is called a Poisson ($\lambda$) random variable ($\lambda > 0$), if $X$ takes
on values $0,1,2,\dots$ and has pmf:
\[
    P(X=k) = \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^k}{k!}, \quad k=0,1,2,\dots
\]
\begin{itemize}
    \item The expected value $E(X) = \lambda$
    \item The variance $V(X) = \lambda$
    \item The parameter $\lambda$ is the average number of occurrences in a given interval.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph*{Note:}
Informally, the smaller $\lambda$ is, the more rare the event $X$ is.

\paragraph*{Example:}
Suppose on average there are two typos per page in the text book.
\begin{itemize}
    \item For a randomly selected page of the book, what is the probability that there are no typos?
    \item For a randomly selected page of the book, what is the probability that there are two typos?
    \item For a randomly selected page of the book, what is the probability that there is at least 1 typo?
\end{itemize}

\pagebreak

Let $X \sim \text{Poisson}(2)$ be the number of typos on a randomly selected page.
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\lambda = 2$
    \item $P(X=0) = \frac{e^{-2} 2  ^0}{0!} = e^{-2} \approx 0.1353$
    \item $P(X=2) = \frac{e^{-2} 2 ^2}{2!} = 2e^{-2} \approx 0.2707$
    \item $P(X \geq 1) = 1 - P(X=0) = 1 - e^{-2} \approx 0.8647$
\end{itemize}

\paragraph*{Example:}
Both Hurricane Harvey and Hurricane Irma were said to be "One in 500 Year" storms, yet they both 
occurred in 2017.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item What is the probability that 2 such storms occur in a given year? Same month?
    \item That is the probability that a baby born today in a hurricane zone will see one such storm.
    What about a "one in 100 year" storm?
\end{enumerate}

Let $X \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda)$ be the number of "one in 500 year" storms in a year and 
$Y \sim \text{Poisson}(\mu)$ be the number of "one in 500 year" storms in a month.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \begin{align*}
        \lambda &= \frac{1}{500} \\
        P(X=2) &= \frac{e^{-\frac{1}{500}} \left(\frac{1}{500}\right)^2}{2!} \\
        &\approx 1.996 \times 10^{-6}\\
        \\
        \mu &= \frac{1}{500 \cdot 12} = \frac{1}{6000} \\
        P(Y=2) &= \frac{e^{-\frac{1}{6000}} \left(\frac{1}{6000}\right)^2}{2!} \\
    \end{align*}
    \item \begin{align*}
        \lambda &= \frac{1}{100} \\
        P(X \geq 1) &= 1 - P(X=0) = 1 - e^{-\frac{1}{100}} \\
        &\approx 0.00995\\
        \\
        \mu &= \frac{1}{100 \cdot 12} = \frac{1}{1200} \\
        P(Y \geq 1) &= 1 - P(Y=0) = 1 - e^{-\frac{1}{1200}} \\
        &\approx 0.000833
    \end{align*}
\end{enumerate}
